Andipatti Animal Feed System
Document 07 — Complete Accounting & Khata Engine Specification
v1.0 — Implementation Baseline
Aligned with PRD v1.2 and Documents 01–06. This is the financial-control baseline for customer Khata, supplier payables, sales, receipts, contra/buy-back, payments, refunds, cash/EOD, journal entries, reconciliation and audit.
1. Purpose
Provide a deterministic financial ledger for every monetary event.
Support customer credit/Khata and supplier payable balances.
Implement double-entry accounting where accounting integration is enabled.
Ensure invoices, payments, returns, contra and EOD are atomic and auditable.
Prevent duplicate financial effects through idempotency.
Separate operational documents from immutable financial postings.
Support offline transaction envelopes without compromising server authority.
2. Financial Principles
Postgres is the authoritative financial source.
Money uses NUMERIC(12,2) minimum and decimal-safe Go/Flutter types.
Posted journals and ledger entries are immutable.
Corrections use reversals, returns, refunds or adjustment documents.
Every posting references a source event/document.
Tenant isolation uses PostgreSQL RLS plus application authorization.
Financial-year and document series are explicit.
Client assertions never establish payment success by themselves.
3. Accounting Model
4. Double-Entry Rules
For every posted journal entry: total debits must equal total credits. A journal may contain multiple lines but must balance exactly to the smallest supported currency unit.
Debits and credits are represented as nonnegative amounts plus side/type.
Do not store ambiguous signed values without a documented convention.
Journal entry cannot be posted with zero lines.
Posted journal cannot be edited.
Reversal references original journal.
5. Suggested Account Classes
Exact chart of accounts is tenant-configurable and must not hardcode tax/legal classifications beyond required system behavior.
6. Source-to-Journal Mapping
Actual account mapping is configurable and must match the tenant's accounting policy and tax treatment.
7. Customer Khata Model
Khata represents an auditable customer subledger, not merely a balance column.
Every debit/credit entry has source type and source ID.
Invoice on credit increases receivable.
Receipt reduces receivable.
Contra credit reduces receivable/increases customer credit.
Returns reduce receivable or create refund/credit according to policy.
Adjustments require permission and reason.
Running balance is a projection; ledger entries are authoritative.
8. Khata Entry Types
9. Credit Limit
Credit limit validation must occur transactionally at finalization.
Read customer credit profile.
Calculate current authoritative outstanding exposure.
Include pending transaction amount according to policy.
Compare with credit limit.
If within limit, continue.
If exceeded, require configured override permission/PIN or reject.
Persist override reason and actor.
Commit sale and ledger atomically.
10. Credit Exposure Formula
Available credit = credit limit − outstanding exposure, subject to configured exclusions/holds. The exact exposure definition must be tenant-configurable and documented. It must not depend solely on a cached mobile balance.
11. Owner/Admin PIN Override
PIN is separately protected from normal password credentials.
Verify against secure backend-controlled representation when online.
Offline override, if allowed, uses a protected local authorization mechanism with later server validation.
Require elevated permission.
Record actor, device, time, requested amount, limit, override reason and resulting decision.
Do not log the PIN.
12. Customer Receipts
Identify customer.
Enter receipt amount and tender.
Validate payment/tender.
Allocate against outstanding invoices or oldest eligible ledger items according to policy.
Create customer ledger credit.
Create accounting journal.
Commit atomically.
Generate receipt.
Sync if originated offline.
13. Payment Allocation
One payment may settle multiple invoices.
One invoice may be settled by multiple payments.
Allocations are separate immutable records.
Allocation total cannot exceed payment amount.
Invoice settled amount cannot exceed invoice receivable/payment due.
Unallocated payment remains an explicit unapplied balance.
Refund reverses the appropriate allocation/payment according to provider and business rules.
14. Invoice Receivable Lifecycle
15. Overpayment Policy
Never silently discard excess payment.
Represent excess as customer credit/unapplied payment or refund according to policy.
Require approval for cash refund where configured.
Maintain full allocation history.
16. Supplier Payables
GRN creates supplier payable according to configured accounting.
Supplier payments reduce payable.
Supplier debit/credit adjustments require source/reason.
Supplier ledger is immutable.
Supplier balance is derived from ledger entries.
Purchase returns reverse inventory/payable as applicable.
17. Contra / Buy-Back Accounting
Contra is a controlled purchase/buy-back flow where a customer/farmer supplies a commodity and receives credit/value.
Capture supplier/customer identity.
Capture product/UOM/quantity and quality data.
Value commodity using configured valuation policy.
Receive inventory through inventory engine.
Create customer credit ledger entry.
Post accounting entry.
Commit inventory + ledger + accounting atomically.
Valuation must not be inferred from retail selling price unless explicitly configured.
18. Contra Valuation
Use a configured purchase/buy-back rate or approved valuation.
Record rate version/effective date.
Record gross/quality adjustments if applicable.
Preserve original valuation on the transaction.
Changes to future rates must not rewrite historical contra transactions.
19. Sales Returns & Refunds
Reference original invoice.
Validate returnable quantity.
Process inventory return.
Reverse revenue/tax according to configured accounting.
Reverse/reduce customer receivable or initiate refund.
Create provider refund if required.
Commit internal return transaction atomically.
Track external refund asynchronously.
20. Refund Rules
Provider refund has its own idempotency key.
Do not create duplicate refunds on retry.
Refund status is pending until provider confirmation where applicable.
Refund failure does not erase the original return.
Cash refunds require authorization according to policy.
21. Multi-Tender Accounting
Tender allocation must sum exactly to invoice grand total, subject to explicit rounding rules.
22. Rounding
Calculate line/tax totals using decimal arithmetic.
Apply documented rounding at the defined boundary.
Store rounded financial amounts used for posting.
Do not recalculate historical totals using changed rounding rules.
Rounding differences, if material/accounting-relevant, use a configured rounding account.
23. GST / Tax Accounting
Tax rates/profiles are configuration-driven and effective-dated.
Tax components are stored separately when required.
Invoice tax lines feed tax liability reporting.
Returns reverse applicable tax postings.
Tax exports must reconcile to invoices and credit/return documents.
Do not hardcode product tax assumptions such as universal 0% or 18%.
24. Cash Session
Open session with opening float.
Record cash sales.
Record cash customer receipts.
Record cash payouts/expenses.
Record authorized cash refunds.
Calculate expected cash.
Count physical cash.
Record variance and reason.
Close session immutably.
25. Expected Cash Formula
Expected closing cash = opening cash + cash inflows − cash outflows, with each included movement type explicitly configured. UPI, credit sales and noncash tenders must not be included as physical cash.
26. EOD Reconciliation
Freeze/close transaction intake according to policy.
Calculate system expected cash.
Enter physical cash count.
Compare expected vs physical.
Calculate variance.
Require reason/approval when threshold exceeded.
Post variance adjustment if authorized.
Close EOD session.
Generate EOD report and sync.
27. Financial Year & Document Series
Maintain explicit financial_year records.
Invoice/GRN/return/receipt series are configurable per tenant/document type.
Prevent duplicate document numbers through database constraints.
Do not derive document sequence from mobile device time.
Offline transactions may receive provisional local identity and server-authoritative document number on synchronization.
28. Journal Posting Service
All application modules call a centralized accounting posting service rather than inserting journal lines directly.
Validate source event.
Resolve account mapping.
Construct journal.
Verify debit = credit.
Attach source reference.
Persist journal header/lines in same transaction.
Return journal ID.
29. Accounting Transaction Boundary
30. Reversal Architecture
Original posted event remains unchanged.
Create reversal referencing original event.
Reversal has its own journal/ledger/movement IDs.
Partial reversal records exact amount/quantity reversed.
Prevent reversing more than original available balance.
Repeated reversal request is idempotent.
31. Audit Trail
Record actor, device, tenant, request ID, source document and timestamp.
Record overrides, adjustments, refunds, reversals and configuration changes.
Record before/after balances where useful.
Never store PIN/password/token values.
Audit history is append-only from the application perspective.
32. Idempotency
Financial POST endpoints require Idempotency-Key.
Store request fingerprint and original response.
Same key/same request returns original result.
Same key/different request returns conflict.
Payment provider IDs are unique.
Webhook event IDs are unique.
33. Offline Financial Transactions
Flutter creates immutable transaction envelope.
Local Khata/payment/invoice state is provisional.
Server validates financial policy at synchronization.
Accepted operation receives authoritative IDs/version.
Duplicate submission adopts original result.
Conflict preserves transaction and requires defined resolution.
Offline local journal projections must never be treated as a replacement for server accounting.

56. Financial Event Model
Recommended event names: SaleFinalized, PaymentCaptured, PaymentRefunded, ReceiptPosted, CustomerLedgerPosted, SupplierPayablePosted, SupplierPaymentPosted, ContraPosted, ReturnPosted, JournalPosted, EODClosed. Events are emitted after successful commit and consumed idempotently.
57. Outbox
Write outbox record in same transaction as financial state.
Worker publishes after commit.
Retry transient delivery errors.
Dead-letter permanent failures.
Consumers use event ID for deduplication.
58. Archival
Financial history follows retention policy, not simply paid+old.
Archive manifest includes source IDs and checksums.
Archive does not alter financial meaning.
Restore testing verifies statement/invoice traceability.
Postgres backups remain primary recovery control.
59. Data Retention
Posted financial records retained according to legal/accounting policy.
Do not hardcode a universal retention period without business/legal confirmation.

Audit and source-document links must remain available for required retention.

61. Transaction Examples
Cash sale: invoice + cash tender + stock movement + revenue/tax + inventory cost posting commit together. Khata sale: invoice + customer receivable + stock movement + revenue/tax + inventory cost. Customer receipt: payment + allocations + customer ledger + cash/bank journal. Contra: inventory receipt + customer credit + valuation/accounting.
62. Detailed Khata Example
Customer outstanding = ₹8,000.
Credit limit = ₹10,000.
New sale = ₹1,500.
Exposure becomes ₹9,500.
Sale is accepted.
Customer later pays ₹2,000.
Receipt allocation reduces outstanding to ₹7,500.
Every event has its own immutable ledger/journal references.
63. Credit Override Example
Customer outstanding ₹9,500, limit ₹10,000, requested credit ₹2,000. Transaction exceeds available credit by ₹1,500. System rejects or requires authorized override. If overridden, actor/reason/amount/limit are recorded. The backend performs the final decision transactionally.
64. Supplier Payable Example
GRN creates a payable of ₹50,000 according to configured purchase accounting. Supplier payment of ₹20,000 is allocated. Outstanding payable becomes ₹30,000. Both events remain separately traceable.
65. EOD Example
Opening cash ₹5,000 + cash sales ₹20,000 + cash receipts ₹4,000 − cash payouts ₹2,500 = expected ₹26,500. Physical count is entered. Any variance is displayed and handled by the configured approval/accounting policy. Noncash UPI/Khata amounts are excluded from physical cash.

73. Final Financial Architecture
The Accounting & Khata Engine provides immutable, auditable financial history with double-entry journal support, customer and supplier subledgers, transactional credit controls, exact payment allocation, contra/buy-back accounting, returns/refunds, cash/EOD reconciliation, tax linkage and idempotent synchronization. Operational projections may exist on Flutter, but PostgreSQL remains the financial authority.

Appendix A — Non-Negotiable Financial Rules
Never use floating-point money arithmetic.
Never directly edit a posted ledger balance.
Never directly edit a posted journal.
Never post an unbalanced journal.
Never trust client payment success alone.
Never exceed credit limit without explicit policy/authorization.
Never duplicate a financial effect on retry.
Never silently erase an overpayment.
Never silently rewrite historical pricing/tax/accounting.
Never use last-write-wins for financial records.
