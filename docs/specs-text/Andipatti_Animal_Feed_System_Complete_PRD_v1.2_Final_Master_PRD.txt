ANDIPATTI ANIMAL FEED SYSTEM
Complete Product Requirements Document (PRD) • Business + Functional + Technical Scope • Version 1.0
Document status: Baseline for architecture, database, API, Flutter UI, integrations, testing and production deployment.
Primary business context: Animal-feed retail/wholesale shop with inventory, batch control, weighing, POS, GST, customer credit (Khata), supplier procurement, payments, hardware, reporting and archival.
Important regulatory note: GST, e-invoicing, e-way bill, HSN/tax-rate, FSSAI and payment-provider requirements must be validated against the applicable current rules and provider specifications before production release. Tax rules must be configuration-driven, not hard-coded.
1. Executive Summary
The Andipatti Animal Feed System is an offline-first, multi-tenant-capable retail/wholesale management platform designed for an animal-feed shop. It covers the complete business lifecycle from shop setup and supplier procurement through goods receipt, batch/expiry control, weighed loose sales, invoicing, split tenders, customer Khata, contra/buy-back transactions, payment confirmation, statutory reporting, end-of-day reconciliation and long-term archival.
The system is designed around a Flutter application for the shop floor and a Go backend with PostgreSQL as the authoritative transactional database. SQLite on the device provides offline execution. Redis/queues may be used for asynchronous jobs. Cloudflare R2 provides low-cost archival storage. Hardware integrations include ESC/POS thermal printers, digital weighing scales and barcode scanners.
The design prioritizes correctness of stock and money, fast billing, tenant isolation, offline continuity, idempotent synchronization, auditable accounting, configurable taxation and operational simplicity.
2. Product Vision and Goals
Reduce billing time and manual entry for animal-feed sales.
Maintain accurate stock across bags, loose kilograms and other UOMs.
Prevent avoidable spoilage through batch, FIFO and expiry controls.
Provide reliable customer credit/Khata with limits, approvals and complete audit history.
Support mixed cash, UPI and credit settlement on one invoice.
Continue selling during internet outages and reconcile safely when connectivity returns.
Automate procurement alerts, payment confirmations, reminders and compliance exports.
Keep the primary database lean while retaining auditable historical archives.
Provide owner/manager visibility through dashboards, reports and reconciliation.
Make all important business rules configurable per tenant and effective-dated.
3. Non-Goals / Boundaries
Not a replacement for a full statutory accounting package unless the accounting module is explicitly enabled.
Not a bank or regulated lending platform; customer credit is trade credit only.
Not an automatic tax-law decision engine; tax classifications require approved master data/configuration.
Not dependent on one payment gateway, printer brand, weighing-scale protocol or messaging provider.
Not permitted to silently delete financial history merely because it is archived; retention, restore and audit policies govern archival.
4. Users, Roles and Permissions
RBAC must be permission-based rather than role-name based. Sensitive actions such as credit-limit override, stock adjustment, invoice cancellation, payment reversal and archival must require explicit permissions and audit records.
5. Tenant / Shop Model
Every business record must be tenant-scoped. tenant_id is mandatory on all tenant-owned tables and must be enforced at the application/service layer, with database-level safeguards where practical.
Shop profile: legal name, trade name, address, GSTIN if applicable, FSSAI/license details if applicable, contact details, invoice prefix, financial year settings and tax registration configuration.
Each device has device_id, tenant_id, device name, app version, last sync time, local sequence state and activation status.
Users belong to tenants; a user may have multiple permissions/roles.
No API may return another tenant's data even when an ID is guessed or supplied manually.
All unique business keys must be tenant-scoped.
6. Module 1 — Master Setup & Configuration
6.1 First-run setup
Create/activate tenant.
Capture legal/shop profile.
Create owner account and secure recovery method.
Configure financial year and invoice numbering.
Create default UOMs and tax profiles.
Create default customer tiers.
Register payment methods.
Pair supported hardware.
Run connectivity and print/scale diagnostics.
Create opening inventory and opening balances only through controlled opening-balance workflows.
6.2 UOM Matrix
UOM must support independent quantity and weight concepts. Parent-child conversion must be explicit and effective-dated.
Use decimal/numeric quantities rather than binary floating point for inventory, weight and currency. Every conversion must retain source UOM, destination UOM, factor and reason/version.
6.3 Product Master
SKU, barcode(s), product name, Tamil/local display name, category, brand, HSN/SAC where applicable, tax profile, purchase UOM, sale UOM, base inventory UOM, pack size, standard weight, MRP, selling price, discount policy, reorder level, reorder quantity, batch/expiry requirement, active status.
Support bagged products, loose products, supplements, additives and services/charges where permitted.
Product aliases support Tamil voice/search and alternate spellings.
Barcode uniqueness must be tenant-scoped unless a global barcode registry is intentionally implemented.
6.4 Customer tiers and pricing
Walk-In, Registered Farmer, Wholesale Dealer, Aavin Sub-Contractor and configurable future tiers.
Tier price rules must be effective-dated.
Discounts may be percentage, fixed amount or product-specific where authorized.
Never allow discounting below configured floor without approval.
Customer-specific price lists can override tier pricing under controlled precedence rules.
7. Module 2 — Supplier & Procurement
7.1 Supplier master
Supplier legal/trade name, GSTIN if applicable, contact, address, payment terms, credit terms, bank/UPI details where required, tax configuration, active status.
Supplier ledger tracks purchases, payments, debit/credit notes and outstanding payable.
7.2 Reorder engine
Trigger by available sellable stock, not merely total physical stock.
Support minimum level, reorder level, target level, lead time and supplier preference.
Exclude expired/quarantined stock from available quantity.
Draft PO automatically; human approval remains configurable.
7.3 Purchase Order
PO number, supplier, lines, requested quantities/UOMs, expected date, price, tax profile, notes, status and approval history.
PO lifecycle: Draft → Approved → Sent → Partially Received → Fully Received → Closed/Cancelled.
PDF generation is asynchronous and versioned; generated document is retained as an attachment/archive reference.
7.4 GRN
GRN is the authoritative event for physically received stock. It must not simply overwrite a product's stock count.
Capture PO reference where present, supplier invoice/challan, vehicle, receiver, date/time, product, batch, manufacturing date, expiry date, received quantity, UOM, gross weight, tare weight, net weight, unit cost, tax and quality status.
Tare can be configured by bag count × standard tare or entered/measured. The system must show the calculation and permit authorized adjustment.
Example: 50 bags × 0.100 kg tare = 5.000 kg deduction. The system must not assume a tare range as an automatic truth; the configured tare method must be explicit.
Rejected/damaged/quarantined goods must be separate stock states and must not become sellable inventory.
GRN posting creates stock movement and supplier payable/accounting events according to configuration.
8. Module 3 — Inventory, Batch and Loss Prevention
8.1 Inventory ledger
Stock must be event-driven. Never rely solely on a mutable products.stock_quantity column as the source of truth.
Stock movement types: Opening, Purchase/GRN, Sale, Sale Return, Purchase Return, Adjustment, Damage, Expiry, Transfer, Conversion, Repack, Contra Receipt, Manual Correction.
Each movement records tenant, product, batch, UOM, quantity, signed quantity, source document, source line, device, user, timestamp and reason.
Current stock may be cached/materialized from movements for performance, but reconciliation must be possible.
8.2 Batch
batch_id, product_id, supplier_id, manufacturing date, expiry date, received date, quantity received, quantity available, cost, status, storage location, quality/quarantine status.
FIFO selection orders eligible stock by receipt date/batch policy. FEFO (first-expiry-first-out) should be supported and may be preferred for perishable feed.
Sale allocation must record exactly which batch quantity was consumed.
8.3 Expiry alerts
Configurable thresholds such as 30/15/7/1 days.
Alert severity based on remaining days and quantity/value at risk.
Daily job creates deduplicated notifications; users can acknowledge/snooze where authorized.
Expired stock automatically becomes non-sellable unless an authorized exception workflow exists.
8.4 Loose/fractional inventory
A 12.5 kg sale from a 50 kg bag must not be implemented as an imprecise floating subtraction. The inventory engine should represent sealed-pack quantity and loose-weight quantity using decimal arithmetic and an explicit conversion/repack model.
Example display: 14 sealed bags + 37.500 kg loose. The exact underlying movement chain must remain auditable.
9. Module 4 — POS & Billing
9.1 Checkout
Identify customer or select Walk-In.
Scan/search product by barcode, SKU, Tamil/local name or voice-assisted search.
For weighed products, read stable weight from scale.
Apply price list/tier/discount rules.
Validate stock and batch allocation.
Calculate tax from effective tax configuration.
Accept one or more tenders.
Validate credit limit if credit is used.
Create invoice transaction atomically.
Print/send receipt.
Post stock and ledger events.
Queue integration events.
9.2 Barcode and Tamil search
Barcode scanner may operate as keyboard/HID or supported SDK.
Search must support Tamil Unicode, transliteration, aliases and fuzzy matching.
Voice search is an optional enhancement; billing must remain fully usable without speech recognition.
Search results should prioritize active products and exact barcode matches.
9.3 Weighing scale
Support Bluetooth/serial/USB adapters according to hardware capability.
Capture stable weight, unit, timestamp and device.
Show live weight and stability indicator.
Reject negative/invalid/out-of-range values.
Record manual override separately from measured weight.
Scale calibration/diagnostics must be an administrative function.
9.4 GST/tax engine
Tax must be data-driven. HSN and rates are master/configuration data subject to applicable law. The invoice engine calculates tax per line/tax component and aggregates it on the invoice.
Support CGST+SGST for intra-state taxable supplies and IGST for applicable inter-state supplies.
Support zero-rated/exempt/non-GST/other configured treatments as applicable.
Do not hard-code one HSN as universally representing animal feed.
Tax profile must contain effective_from/effective_to, rate components, classification, rounding policy and applicability conditions.
Invoice stores the tax snapshot used at issuance so later master-data changes do not rewrite historical invoices.
Tax rounding must be deterministic and tested for line-level and invoice-level scenarios.
9.5 Invoice numbering
Financial-year-aware numbering.
Tenant-specific prefix/series.
Device-safe allocation for offline mode.
No duplicate numbers after synchronization.
Cancelled numbers remain auditable and are never silently reused.
9.6 Multi-tender
Example ₹3,000 bill: ₹1,000 cash + ₹500 UPI + ₹1,500 credit. Tender lines must sum exactly to invoice grand total. Credit tender posts a customer receivable; cash/UPI posts the corresponding settlement account.
9.7 Returns and cancellations
Sales return references original invoice/line where possible.
Return quantity cannot exceed eligible sold quantity unless an authorized exception is used.
Refund can be cash, UPI reversal/refund, credit to Khata or configurable combination.
Invoice cancellation requires reason, permission and immutable audit record.
After statutory submission/locking, corrections should use the appropriate adjustment/credit-note workflow rather than destructive edits.
10. Module 5 — Udhari / Khata / Trade Credit
10.1 Customer ledger
The customer balance must be derived from immutable financial events: credit sales, receipts, returns, adjustments and approved contra transactions.
Credit limit, available credit, outstanding balance, overdue balance, payment terms, risk/status and authorized override policy.
At POS, calculate proposed post-transaction balance before posting.
Over-limit credit requires configured owner/manager approval and records approver identity/reason.
10.2 Receipts
Cash/UPI/bank receipt against one or multiple invoices.
Unapplied receipt support where business permits.
Receipt reversal requires permission and reason.
Customer statement shows opening, transactions, receipts, adjustments and closing balance.
10.3 Contra / buy-back
A farmer may provide approved raw material in exchange for reducing an outstanding feed balance. This is not merely a negative payment; it is an inventory receipt plus a financial settlement.
Create buy-back/contra document with product, batch/quality, quantity, valuation, approval and ledger allocation.
Debit inventory/stock value and credit the appropriate contra/settlement account according to accounting configuration.
Apply approved value against customer receivable.
Never permit arbitrary valuation without an authorized price/approval rule.
10.4 Aavin-cycle reminders
Reminder schedule is configurable rather than permanently tied to the 10th/25th.
Only eligible customers with outstanding balance are selected.
Message templates support Tamil and English.
WhatsApp provider response/status is recorded.
UPI payment link is generated only through a configured payment/collection mechanism.
Opt-out, consent and messaging compliance rules must be respected.
11. Module 6 — Payments & Hardware
11.1 UPI/payment integration
Use provider abstraction so the system can support multiple gateways/collect mechanisms.
Webhook must verify provider signature/authentication.
Webhook processing must be idempotent.
Payment state machine: Created → Pending → Authorized/Success → Failed/Expired/Refunded/Reversed.
Do not mark an invoice paid solely because a client says payment succeeded.
Reconcile provider settlement against internal transactions.
11.2 Soundbox
Soundbox status is supplemental confirmation, not the accounting source of truth.
Provider webhook or trusted payment confirmation remains authoritative.
Handle delayed/out-of-order webhook events.
11.3 ESC/POS
Receipt renderer produces raw ESC/POS commands where supported.
Configurable paper width, character set, Tamil support strategy, logo, QR, GST fields, footer and cut command.
Printer failure must not silently roll back a successful invoice.
Reprint is an explicit operation and is audited.
12. Module 7 — Compliance & Reporting
12.1 E-way bill
The system should evaluate e-way-bill applicability using configurable legal rules and the latest authorized integration specification. It must collect required transaction, consignee, transporter and vehicle data when applicable. Do not hard-code a single threshold as the only rule.
12.2 EOD reconciliation
At close, expected drawer cash is calculated from opening cash plus cash receipts less cash payouts/withdrawals and other configured cash movements. The manager enters physical cash. Difference is logged as short/over with reason and approval.
Locking EOD prevents unauthorized backdated changes.
Late transactions require controlled reopen/adjustment workflow.
EOD report includes sales by tender, refunds, expenses/payouts, credit sales, expected cash, actual cash and variance.
12.3 GST exports
Export formats must be configuration-driven and aligned with the current portal/accounting import requirements.
Separate B2B/B2C and applicable tax categories.
Include invoice number/date, customer GSTIN where applicable, place of supply, taxable values, tax amounts, HSN and document status.
Export is a reporting artifact; it does not itself prove statutory filing success.
Reconciliation report flags missing/invalid GSTIN, tax mismatch, duplicate invoice, cancellation and credit-note issues.
13. Module 8 — Archival & Retention
Archival is a storage-management feature, not permission to destroy the only copy of financial records.
Archive candidates based on configurable retention policy, financial year, settlement state and legal/accounting requirements.
Before purge, create a versioned archive package containing data, metadata, checksums, schema version, export manifest and generation timestamp.
Upload to Cloudflare R2/S3-compatible storage using encryption and restricted credentials.
Verify upload and checksum before any purge.
Maintain archive_catalog in PostgreSQL with object key, checksum, row counts, date range, schema version, encryption metadata and restore status.
Prefer soft-delete/partitioning or cold-storage strategies over irreversible DELETE when retention requirements are uncertain.
Any purge requires an explicit policy, audit trail and tested restore process.
Never purge records solely because payment_status='Fully Paid'.
Recommended architecture: partition high-volume transaction tables by financial year/month where appropriate; archive old partitions/records after retention review; retain summarized balances and archive references in the live database.
14. Offline-First Architecture
14.1 Local data
SQLite stores only the data required for the device's assigned tenant, users and operational horizon.
Use encrypted storage/secure key handling where feasible.
Local database includes products, prices, customers, credit limits, tax snapshots/config, inventory availability snapshot, pending invoices, payments and sync metadata.
14.2 Transaction envelope
Every offline write receives a globally unique client_transaction_id, device_id, local_sequence, created_at, entity_type, payload_version and idempotency key.
14.3 Sync
Device creates local transaction.
Transaction is committed locally before UI success is shown.
Sync worker uploads pending envelopes in order where ordering matters.
Server validates tenant, authorization, version and business rules.
Server accepts idempotently or returns the original result for duplicate submissions.
Server assigns authoritative IDs/timestamps where appropriate.
Device marks transaction acknowledged.
Conflicts are routed to a deterministic resolution workflow; never silently overwrite financial data.
14.4 Offline restrictions
Some operations may be disallowed offline: high-risk refunds, statutory API submissions, certain credit overrides, master-data changes and payment confirmation.
Offline credit should use a cached credit limit with a configurable safety buffer and risk policy.
Invoice numbering must be designed specifically for offline concurrency.
15. API Requirements — Go Backend
Use versioned REST APIs, e.g. /api/v1, with OpenAPI documentation. WebSockets are for realtime events, not the authoritative transaction channel.
All write endpoints must support authorization, tenant isolation, validation, idempotency where applicable, audit logging and structured errors.

PRD v1.1 — Technical Hardening Addendum
This addendum incorporates the requested security, offline-storage, numeric-precision, local-language search, collection-reminder, hardware-fallback and GRN tare-validation controls. These requirements are mandatory for the production design and supersede weaker/less-specific wording elsewhere in v1.0.
A1. Database-Level Tenant Isolation — PostgreSQL RLS
PostgreSQL Row-Level Security (RLS) is mandatory for every tenant-owned transactional table in production.
The Go API must establish the authenticated tenant context on every database transaction using a trusted server-side session variable or equivalent mechanism; clients must never be allowed to choose an arbitrary tenant context.
RLS policies must deny access by default and allow rows only when row.tenant_id matches the trusted transaction tenant context.
Service accounts that require cross-tenant administration must use an explicitly separate, tightly permissioned administrative path; ordinary application connections must not bypass RLS.
Automated security tests must attempt cross-tenant reads, inserts, updates and deletes using valid object IDs and verify denial.
Migrations must fail CI/review if a new tenant-owned table is created without tenant_id, the required index and an RLS policy.
Application-layer tenant checks remain mandatory. RLS is a defense-in-depth failsafe, not a replacement for authorization.
A2. Encrypted Offline SQLite — SQLCipher
The Flutter offline database must use SQLCipher (or an equivalent audited encrypted SQLite implementation) rather than plaintext SQLite.
The database encryption key must never be hard-coded, embedded in the APK/IPA, stored in source control or stored as plaintext in shared preferences.
The key must be generated/managed through the platform secure keystore/keychain facilities, with appropriate device binding and recovery policy.
Sensitive local data includes customer Khata balances/limits, customer identifiers, cached pricing, pending payment/sync envelopes, device credentials and other business data; all such data is protected by the encrypted database.
Pending offline transaction envelopes must include integrity protection so a modified local record is rejected during synchronization.
On device logout/deactivation, the application must revoke access and follow the configured secure-data lifecycle policy.
Rooted/jailbroken devices should be treated as higher risk; the system may restrict offline operation based on an explicit device-security policy.
A3. Standardized Numeric Precision
All API, Go, PostgreSQL and Flutter serialization rules must use a single documented numeric model. JSON monetary and quantity values must not be transported through binary floating-point types when precision could affect financial or inventory correctness.
The exact precision may be increased if future products require it, but the project must not mix incompatible scales for the same business concept. Database migrations, OpenAPI schemas, validation and serialization tests must enforce the chosen scale.
A4. Phonetic / Colloquial Tamil Alias Engine
product_aliases must support Tamil Unicode, English, transliteration, phonetic spellings, colloquial regional terms, abbreviations and common farmer shorthand.
Each alias should record alias_text, normalized_text, language/script, alias_type, optional region/context, priority and active status.
Search normalization should handle Unicode normalization, punctuation/spacing differences and configured transliteration equivalents.
The system must support multiple aliases pointing to the same SKU and must preserve the canonical product name separately.
Search ranking should prioritize exact barcode/SKU, exact canonical name, exact alias, normalized alias and fuzzy/phonetic matches in that order.
Voice search results must always be confirmed by the worker before an irreversible billing action if confidence is below the configured threshold.
Administrators must be able to add shop-specific aliases without changing the canonical product master.
A5. Khata Collection Reminder Grace Period
Add tenant configuration: collection_cycle_dates, reminder_days_before, grace_period_days, reminder_pause_dates and reminder_pause_reason.
Grace period must suppress or postpone collection reminders for the configured number of days after the expected cooperative payout date.
The reminder engine must also support ad-hoc local payout delays without changing the underlying customer ledger due date.
A reminder suppressed by a grace period must be visible in an internal notification/audit log with the reason.
After the grace period expires, only eligible outstanding balances should be selected.
Customers must not receive duplicate reminders for the same cycle.
Message templates must support respectful Tamil wording and configurable payment-link text.
A6. Printer Failure — Immediate WhatsApp Receipt Fallback
Finalized invoice creation must complete independently of printer availability.
If ESC/POS printing returns timeout, disconnect, paper-out or other configured failure, the POS must immediately display a non-blocking failure state with a one-tap 'Send receipt via WhatsApp' option.
The WhatsApp fallback must use the finalized invoice data, not recalculate the bill.
Receipt delivery must be asynchronous and must show Pending/Sent/Failed status.
If the customer has no WhatsApp-capable number, the UI must offer reprint or other configured receipt options.
A printer retry must never create a second invoice; it is a reprint operation tied to the original invoice ID.
WhatsApp delivery failure must never change the financial state of the invoice.
A7. GRN Tare Weight Validation & Approval
The GRN must calculate gross weight, configured tare weight and resulting net weight separately and display the complete calculation before posting.
A configurable maximum tare percentage must be enforced against gross weight. Recommended initial default: 5%, subject to validation with the actual bag types used by the business.
The threshold must be configurable by product/package type because tare characteristics can differ.
If entered tare exceeds the configured threshold, the GRN cannot be posted normally. The user must correct the value or obtain an authorized override.
Override requires a permitted role, explicit reason and audit record containing gross, tare, threshold and final net weight.
Net weight must never be negative. Zero or implausibly low net weight must trigger validation.
For count-based tare, the system must calculate tare from bag count × configured tare per bag and show both the formula and result.
Where a physical scale is available, measured tare should be stored separately from standard tare.
A8. Additional Acceptance Tests for v1.1
A cashier authenticated for Tenant A cannot read or modify a Tenant B invoice even when the Tenant B invoice ID is known.
A database query executed without the trusted tenant context returns no tenant-owned rows.
A rooted-device tampering attempt against a pending local sync record is detected and rejected.
₹123.45 remains exactly ₹123.45 through Flutter → JSON → Go → PostgreSQL → JSON.
12.345 kg remains exact through POS → API → inventory movement → report.
A colloquial Tamil alias resolves to the intended SKU while the canonical product name remains unchanged.
A 2-day cooperative payout delay suppresses configured collection reminders without changing the customer's actual receivable.
A printer timeout offers WhatsApp receipt fallback and does not create a duplicate invoice.
A GRN with tare above the configured maximum is blocked until corrected or explicitly overridden.
Every tare override contains user, device, timestamp, reason and before/after values in the audit trail.
A9. Updated Mandatory Architecture Principles
PostgreSQL is the authoritative transactional system.
PostgreSQL RLS is the final database-level tenant-isolation control.
Flutter uses SQLCipher for encrypted offline storage.
Money uses NUMERIC(12,2) or a higher approved precision; weights use NUMERIC(12,3); UOM factors use NUMERIC(12,6).
Inventory remains event-driven and auditable.
Offline writes are idempotent and integrity-protected.
Tax/HSN rules are effective-dated configuration snapshots, never hard-coded business assumptions.

PRD v1.2 — Final Master Clarifications & Production Controls
This section consolidates additional production controls so the PRD can be used as the master requirements baseline for database, backend, Flutter, hardware, integrations, QA and deployment.
A10. Mandatory Transaction Atomicity
Invoice finalization, invoice lines, tax snapshot, tender allocation, stock movements, batch allocation and customer ledger entries must be committed atomically in one authoritative server-side transaction whenever the operation is online.
An invoice must never exist as 'successful' while its required stock or financial postings are only partially committed.
Offline transactions are first committed atomically to the local SQLCipher database and subsequently committed atomically by the server.
External side effects such as printer output, WhatsApp delivery and provider notifications must occur after the financial transaction is committed and must be independently retryable.
Every transaction must have a correlation ID so invoice, payment, stock, ledger, sync and audit records can be traced together.
A11. Idempotency & Duplicate Prevention
Every financially material API command must accept or derive an idempotency key.
The server must persist the idempotency result for the applicable retention period and return the original result for duplicate requests.
Idempotency must cover offline invoice upload, payment creation, payment webhook processing, refunds, receipts, contra entries and stock adjustments.
Client retries after timeout must never create a second invoice, payment, receipt or ledger posting.
Idempotency keys must be scoped to tenant and operation type.
A12. Concurrency & Stock Reservation
When multiple POS devices sell the same product, the server must prevent overselling through transactional stock validation/locking or an equivalent reservation mechanism.
Offline devices may operate against cached stock only under a configurable offline-risk policy.
On synchronization, insufficient-stock conflicts must be explicitly resolved and must never silently create negative stock.
Inventory balance calculations must be safe under concurrent GRNs, sales, returns and adjustments.
A13. Financial Year & Number-Series Control
Financial year configuration must be effective-dated and immutable for finalized documents.
Invoice, PO, GRN, receipt, return, credit-note and contra series must be independently configurable where required.
Offline numbering must be collision-safe across all active devices.
Number gaps caused by failed/cancelled documents must remain auditable.
Year-end transition must preserve historical numbering and create the next year's series without rewriting prior documents.
A14. Master-Data Governance
Product, UOM, tax, pricing, customer tier, credit-limit and supplier-master changes must have effective dates.
Historical transactions retain immutable snapshots of relevant master values.
Deleting master records is prohibited when referenced by historical transactions; records are deactivated instead.
Critical master changes may require approval according to tenant policy.
Bulk imports must run validation, duplicate detection and preview before commit.
A15. Stock Count & Shrinkage Workflow
Physical stock counts must support full-shop and cycle-count modes.
Count sessions capture expected quantity, counted quantity, variance, reason and approver.
Variance does not directly overwrite stock; it creates an auditable adjustment movement.
Large variances can require manager/owner approval based on configurable percentage or value thresholds.
Batch-level counting must be supported for controlled products.
Count reports must show book quantity, physical quantity, variance quantity and variance value.
A16. Product Costing & Margin
The system must support a configurable inventory-costing method, with FIFO/weighted-average behavior explicitly selected by accounting policy.
Purchase cost, landed cost and selling price must remain distinguishable.
Gross-margin reports must state the costing method used.
Historical cost used for a sale must be traceable to the relevant inventory allocation/cost layer.
Discounts, returns, purchase price variance and applicable charges must be handled consistently in margin reporting.
A17. Returns, Refunds & Credit Notes
Sales returns must reference the original invoice whenever possible.
Returned goods require condition classification: sellable, damaged, expired, quarantine or other configured status.
Refunds must be linked to the return and original payment allocation.
Refunds and credit notes require appropriate authorization and audit logging.
Statutory documents already reported externally must not be silently edited; corrective workflows must be used.
A18. Payment Reconciliation
Internal payment records and provider settlement records must be reconciled.
Reconciliation must identify unmatched internal payments, unmatched provider payments, amount mismatches, duplicate events, delayed settlements and refunds.
Manual reconciliation adjustments require approval and audit.
Payment provider settlement files/reports may be imported where supported.
Payment success at POS and final settlement are separate states when the provider model requires it.
A19. WhatsApp Messaging Governance
Store only the minimum messaging data required for business operation.
Message templates must be versioned and associated with a purpose.
Delivery states must include queued, sent, delivered, read where the provider supplies them, failed and suppressed.
Retries must use exponential backoff and must not generate duplicate customer messages.
Opt-out and provider/template restrictions must be honored.
WhatsApp is an optional communication channel; core billing and ledger operation must not depend on it.
A20. Device & Hardware Lifecycle
Each tablet/device must have a registered device identity and tenant association.
Hardware pairing must be revocable.
Printer and scale configurations must be versioned and testable.
Device replacement must provide a controlled migration/deactivation workflow.
Lost/stolen/decommissioned devices must be remotely disabled from synchronization.
Hardware diagnostics should expose connection status, last successful operation and error code where available.
A21. API Error Contract
All APIs must return a consistent error envelope containing machine-readable code, human-readable message, correlation ID and validation details where applicable.
Errors must distinguish authentication, authorization, validation, conflict, business-rule rejection, dependency failure and server failure.
Sensitive internal exception details must never be returned to the client.
Retryable errors must be explicitly identifiable.
API versions must remain backward compatible for the supported client versions or provide a documented migration path.
A22. Import / Export Safety
CSV/Excel imports must validate file type, size, encoding, headers, row count and field constraints before processing.
Large imports must be asynchronous with progress and downloadable validation-error reports.
Exports containing sensitive data must respect RBAC and tenant scope.
Generated exports must have an audit record and configurable retention.
Never execute formulas/macros from imported spreadsheet content.
A23. Search & Localization
All user-facing labels should support English and Tamil localization.
Dates, currency and number formats must follow tenant locale settings.
Search normalization must preserve original stored text.
Reports and receipts must support Tamil rendering where the selected printer/font path supports it.
Localization must not alter underlying canonical codes, IDs, HSN values, invoice numbers or accounting values.
A24. Accessibility & Usability
POS primary actions must be reachable with minimal taps.
Large touch targets and high-contrast states should be used for shop-floor conditions.
Critical errors must explain the action required rather than expose technical stack traces.
Offline status must be continuously visible but unobtrusive.
Payment, credit and stock warnings must be visually distinct and require acknowledgment where risk is material.
A25. Release & Change Management
Database migrations must be version-controlled, reversible where practical and tested against representative production-size data.
API, Flutter and database versions must have compatibility rules.
Production deployment requires automated backup verification and health checks.
Feature flags should allow risky integrations to be enabled gradually.
Every production release must have a rollback plan.
Security-sensitive changes require code review and targeted regression testing.

A28. Final Master Architecture Statement
The Andipatti Animal Feed System shall be implemented as a security-first, offline-first, event-driven business platform. PostgreSQL is the authoritative transactional store and uses Row-Level Security for tenant isolation. Flutter uses SQLCipher for protected offline operation. Go provides the business-rule and API layer. Inventory and financial events are immutable/auditable where practical, while materialized balances are performance projections. External integrations are asynchronous and idempotent. Printing and messaging are delivery channels and never determine whether a financial transaction succeeded. Archival is governed by retention policy, integrity verification, backup and restore testing.
