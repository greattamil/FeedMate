Document 06 — Complete Inventory & Batch Engine Specification
v1.0 — Implementation Baseline
Aligned with PRD v1.2, Database & ERD v1.0, API Specification v1.0, Go Backend Technical Architecture v1.0, Flutter Technical Architecture v1.0, and Offline-First & Synchronization Specification v1.0.
This document defines the authoritative inventory engine: UOM conversion, receiving, tare validation, batches, stock ledger, balances, FIFO/FEFO allocation, loose/fraction stock, reservations, counts, adjustments, expiry/aflatoxin controls, costing, concurrency, reconciliation, offline interaction, and audit.
1. Inventory Engine Objectives
Maintain an exact, auditable quantity trail for every stock movement.
Support bags, kilograms, tonnes, loose/fraction quantities and configurable product UOMs.
Track batches where batch/expiry/quality controls apply.
Allocate stock deterministically using configured FIFO/FEFO policies.
Prevent double allocation and unauthorized negative stock.
Separate physical quantity from accounting/cost information.
Support purchase receiving, sales, returns, adjustments, stock counts and contra/buy-back.
Remain safe under concurrent devices and offline synchronization.
Make expiry, quality and shrinkage visible before they become financial losses.
2. Core Inventory Concepts
3. Authoritative Inventory Model
stock_movements is the audit ledger. stock_balances is a derived/current-state projection. The engine must be able to rebuild balances from movements or otherwise reconcile them.
Every mutation creates a movement or an explicit movement-linked state transition.
Balances are updated atomically with movements.
Finalized movements are immutable.
Corrections are compensating movements.
All quantities are decimal-safe NUMERIC values.
Tenant, product, UOM, location and batch dimensions are explicit.
4. Precision Standards
Go must use decimal-safe types.
Flutter must preserve decimal values without binary floating-point conversion.
Rounding rules are explicit and consistent.
Never truncate fractional quantities during conversion.
5. UOM Architecture
The system supports parent/child UOM relationships without assuming that every product uses the same package weight.
UOM conversions must be effective-dated when historical correctness requires it.
Product-specific conversions override generic conversions where applicable.
Conversions must declare direction and factor clearly.
Do not hardcode 50 kg for every bag.
6. Product UOM Rules
Each product has a base stock UOM.
Sellable/purchasable UOMs map to the base UOM.
Conversion factors are positive.
Pack size and nominal weight are distinct from actual received weight.
Fractional sale eligibility is configured per product/UOM.
Inventory quantities are stored in a canonical base quantity plus transaction UOM metadata where needed.
7. Conversion Algorithm
For a quantity Q in source UOM with factor F to base UOM: base_quantity = Q × F. For reverse display conversion: source_quantity = base_quantity ÷ F, with configured rounding/display precision.
Validate factor > 0.
Use decimal arithmetic.
Record source UOM and factor/version used for the transaction.
Do not retroactively recalculate historical movements when a conversion changes.
8. Batch Model
Batch number may be mandatory for configured products.
Duplicate batch numbers are permitted only according to tenant/product policy.
Batch identity must not be silently merged when traceability would be lost.
9. Batch Lifecycle
EXPECTED — referenced by PO or advance information.
RECEIVING — physically being received.
AVAILABLE — passed required validations and posted to stock.
HOLD — blocked pending quality/document review.
REJECTED — not available for sale/use.
EXPIRED — expiry reached according to policy.
DEPLETED — no available quantity remains.
ARCHIVED — retained for historical traceability.
A batch may transition between operational statuses only through authorized rules. Expired does not necessarily mean physically destroyed; it means the batch is unavailable for normal sale unless an explicitly authorized policy says otherwise.
10. Quality / Aflatoxin Controls
Quality status is configuration-driven.
Products requiring quality inspection can enter HOLD on receipt.
Quality result/inspection metadata may be attached to GRN/batch.
Daily jobs identify approaching expiry and configured quality-risk conditions.
An alert does not automatically modify stock quantity.
Rejected/held stock is excluded from normal sale allocation.
11. Expiry Policy
The example of 15 days is a configurable initial default, not a universal legal requirement.
12. FIFO Policy
FIFO selects the oldest eligible stock based on the configured inventory-age key, normally received/available date, with deterministic tie-breakers.
Exclude expired/blocked/held batches unless policy explicitly permits.
Sort by eligible age ascending.
Use batch ID or another stable key as tie-breaker.
Allocate only available quantity.
Split allocation across multiple batches when one batch is insufficient.
13. FEFO Policy
FEFO selects stock by earliest expiry date first, then deterministic age/tie-breaker.
Only applicable to products with expiry/quality semantics.
Null expiry handling must be explicit; typically place no-expiry stock after dated stock.
Expired stock is excluded.
FEFO can be the preferred policy while FIFO remains the fallback for non-expiring products.
14. Allocation Policy Configuration
Each product/category policy must resolve to one deterministic allocation algorithm.
15. Stock Movement Types
16. Movement Invariants
Movement quantity must be positive; direction determines sign/effect.
Every movement has a reason/source type and source ID where applicable.
Tenant/product/location scope must be valid.
Batch is mandatory where product policy requires it.
UOM conversion is valid for the product.
Movement cannot be silently deleted after posting.
Reverse/correct using a compensating movement.
17. Stock Balance Model
A stock balance row represents current on-hand quantity for a unique tenant + location + product + batch + UOM/base dimension as defined by the physical schema.
Use a unique composite key for the balance dimension.
Maintain on_hand, reserved and available quantities where reservations are supported.
Available = on_hand − reserved, subject to policy.
Do not let available become negative.
Balance updates occur in the same DB transaction as movements.
18. Reservation Model
Reservations are optional for this retail system but useful for concurrent POS/online operations.
Reservation has owner/source transaction, quantity, status and expiration where applicable.
Reserved quantity cannot exceed eligible on-hand.
Finalization consumes reservation; cancellation releases it.
Expired reservations are released by a controlled job.
Reservations never replace the immutable stock ledger.
19. Concurrent Stock Allocation
Begin database transaction.
Identify all eligible balance/batch rows.
Lock candidate rows in deterministic order.
Calculate available quantity.
Allocate required quantity across candidates.
Insert allocation records/movements.
Update balances atomically.
Commit.
Return allocation snapshot.
Use row-level locking such as SELECT ... FOR UPDATE as appropriate. Application-memory locks are not sufficient for multiple API instances/devices.

Recommended default: prevent negative authoritative stock.

Capture package/bag count.
Capture gross weight.
Capture tare weight.

Create/resolve batch.
Validate expiry/quality.

Generate receipt/GRN result.
Queue synchronization if originating offline.
22. Tare Weight Validation
Tare validation must protect against obvious data-entry/device errors while remaining configurable.
Store gross, tare and net separately.
Net = gross − tare using decimal arithmetic.
Net must not be negative.
Compare total tare against configured maximum percentage or configured absolute rule.
Recommended initial default threshold: 5% of gross, configurable by product/package and not a universal hardcoded rule.
Over-threshold receipt requires correction or authorized override.
Record threshold, actual percentage, decision and reason in audit data.
23. Tare Example
If a truck reports 50 bags and each sack's tare is expected to be approximately 100–150 g, the engine should calculate from the actual configured/entered tare policy. It must not blindly assume a single sack tare value. If 50 bags have 0.125 kg average tare, total tare would be 6.250 kg; gross/net calculations must use actual recorded values and configured validation.
24. Loose / Fraction Stock
Store loose quantity in base UOM with decimal precision.
Example: 12.500 kg remaining from a 50 kg bag.
Display can show sealed bags + loose kg through a conversion/display service.
Do not physically invent a new bag when loose quantity is created.
Splitting a bag into loose stock is represented by linked movements if physical traceability is required.
Sale of loose quantity reduces the appropriate batch/base quantity.
25. Bag-to-Loose Conversion
When a 50 kg bag is opened and 12.500 kg is sold, the system may maintain 37.500 kg remaining as loose/base stock or represent package/loose states explicitly. The chosen representation must be consistent per product and must not cause double counting.
Physical package state and accounting quantity are separate concepts.
Use one canonical quantity to avoid duplicate inventory.
Display sealed/loose is a derived operational view unless package-level traceability requires separate records.
26. Stock Transfer
Transfers use linked OUT and IN movements.
Both sides are committed atomically when within the same inventory domain.
Source and destination locations are required.
Batch identity is preserved unless repack/reclassification is explicitly authorized.
Failed transfer leaves both locations unchanged.
27. Stock Count Workflow
Open count session.
Freeze or snapshot relevant expected quantity according to policy.
Record physical quantities by product/location/batch.
Calculate variance.
Require reason for variance.
Require approval if configured threshold is exceeded.
Create adjustment movements.
Close count.
Reconcile balance.
Counting should minimize disruption to POS. Where stock continues moving during a count, the system must use a documented snapshot/reconciliation method rather than mixing incompatible timestamps.
28. Stock Adjustment Rules
Adjustment requires reason code.
Manual quantity change requires permission.
Large variance may require owner/admin approval.
Before/after values are recorded.
Adjustment links to the count or source reason.
Adjustment creates immutable movement(s).
Never edit stock balance directly as a business operation.
29. Shrinkage / Damage / Expiry
30. Returns
Validate original invoice and returnable quantity.
Return to original batch where traceability permits.
If original batch is unavailable, require policy-based alternative batch disposition.
Returned product may enter HOLD pending quality inspection.
Saleable returned stock increases available quantity only after acceptance.
Refund/credit accounting is separate but linked to stock reversal.
31. Contra / Buy-Back
Contra receipt increases inventory quantity.
Product, UOM, quantity and valuation are explicitly captured.
Quality/batch rules apply if configured.
Customer ledger receives the corresponding credit/valuation.
Inventory and ledger postings are atomic on the server.
Offline contra follows the same synchronization/idempotency rules.
32. Product Costing
Inventory quantity and cost are related but distinct. The system should support a documented costing method.
Choose one authoritative costing method per tenant/business policy and do not mix methods implicitly. Costing changes require effective dating and historical preservation.
33. Margin Calculation
Revenue = finalized sale line net amount according to accounting definition.
Cost = allocated inventory cost.
Gross margin = revenue − cost.
Margin reports must identify whether tax is excluded/included according to configuration.
Discounts must be reflected consistently.
Do not use current replacement cost to rewrite historical sale margin.
34. Expiry Alert Job
Run daily at tenant-local configured time.
Find eligible batches within warning horizon.
Exclude depleted/archived batches.
Group alerts by product/location/batch.
Create deduplicated notification jobs.
Escalate expired stock according to policy.
Do not modify stock solely because an alert is generated.
35. FIFO/FEFO Allocation Pseudocode
1. Resolve product inventory policy.
2. Query eligible batches for tenant/location/product.
3. Filter quality_status = AVAILABLE and quantity_available > 0.
4. Exclude expired stock.
5. Sort by FEFO or FIFO key.
6. For each batch:
   allocate = min(remaining_required, available)
   append allocation
   remaining_required -= allocate
7. If remaining_required > 0:
   apply negative-stock/offline policy.
8. Persist allocations and movements atomically.
The actual implementation must use parameterized/generated SQL and row locks where required.

69. End-to-End Example — FEFO
Eligible batches: A expires first with 10 kg; B expires later with 30 kg. A sale requires 25 kg. Engine allocates 10 kg from A and 15 kg from B, preserving traceability and remaining quantities.
70. End-to-End Example — Concurrent Sale
Two POS devices request 30 kg from a batch containing 40 kg. The database locks the relevant balance/batch rows. The first transaction allocates 30 kg. The second sees 10 kg available and either allocates 10 kg plus another eligible batch or rejects/conflicts according to policy. It cannot create 30 kg of duplicate allocation.

75. Final Inventory Architecture
The inventory engine is ledger-driven, batch-aware, UOM-safe and concurrency-safe. PostgreSQL remains authoritative, while Flutter maintains an encrypted local projection for supported offline operations. FIFO/FEFO allocation is deterministic, all quantities use decimal precision, GRN tare validation is configurable, loose stock is represented without double counting, and all corrections occur through immutable movements. Reconciliation and audit are designed to detect rather than hide discrepancies.

Appendix A — Non-Negotiable Inventory Rules
Never directly edit stock balance as a business correction.
Never use float for inventory quantity.
Never allocate expired/held stock under normal policy.
Never over-allocate due to concurrent requests.
Never silently change historical UOM conversion.
Never hardcode universal bag weight.
Never approve excessive tare without an auditable reason.
Never delete posted movement history.
Never resolve inventory sync conflict using last-write-wins.
Never allow a client to bypass backend stock authority.
