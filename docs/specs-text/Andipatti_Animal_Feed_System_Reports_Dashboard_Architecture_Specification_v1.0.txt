Andipatti Animal Feed System
Document 16 — Complete Reports & Dashboard Architecture / Specification v1.0
Implementation-ready reporting, analytics, dashboard, export, reconciliation and operational intelligence specification for the animal-feed retail/wholesale system. Covers owner, manager, cashier, accounts, inventory, procurement, compliance and audit reporting. No sprint planning.
Authoritative principle: reports are derived from PostgreSQL transactional data and approved projections; they never become an alternate source of financial or inventory truth.
1. Purpose, Scope, Objectives & Non-Goals
1.1 Purpose
Define a complete reporting layer that gives operators accurate, tenant-isolated, traceable and performant visibility into sales, inventory, procurement, customers, Khata, payments, accounting, GST/compliance, hardware, synchronization and EOD.
1.2 Objectives
Provide fast operational dashboards for daily shop decisions.
Provide detailed drill-down reports with reproducible filters.
Reconcile sales, payments, inventory and accounting.
Support Tamil/English labels and localized display formatting.
Support asynchronous large exports without blocking transactional workloads.
Protect PII and sensitive financial information through RBAC and audit.
Make every report traceable to an authoritative record and reporting period.
1.3 Non-Goals
This document does not redefine accounting or tax rules from Documents 07 and 11.
Reports do not authorize financial or inventory mutations.
Predictive analytics are optional extensions; deterministic operational reports are mandatory.
2. Reporting Principles
PostgreSQL transactional data is authoritative.
Tenant isolation applies to every query, view, materialized view and export.
Financial totals use exact decimal calculations consistent with the transaction engine.
Every report states its period, timezone and filter context.
Closed/locked financial periods remain reproducible.
Reports distinguish posted/finalized data from drafts, pending and failed records.
Cancelled, returned and reversed records follow explicit inclusion rules.
Large reports run asynchronously.
Exports are permission-controlled and audited.
Dashboard summaries must reconcile with drill-down totals.
3. Reporting Architecture
Reporting queries must use read-optimized views/indexes where appropriate but preserve authoritative reconciliation paths.
4. Report Security & Tenant Isolation
Every report request is tenant-scoped from authenticated context.
Go authorization is applied before query execution.
PostgreSQL RLS remains mandatory for tenant-owned reporting sources.
Support/auditor cross-tenant views require explicit scoped access.
Export URLs are short-lived and resource-authorized.
Report files are private and stored under server-generated object keys.
PII fields are minimized or masked according to role.
Every sensitive export is audited with actor, tenant, filters, period and file reference.
5. Reporting Period & Timezone Rules
Store authoritative timestamps consistently, preferably UTC, with tenant/shop timezone for presentation.
Daily reports use tenant-local calendar dates.
Financial-year reports use configured financial-year boundaries.
Month-end and GST reports use explicit period start/end.
Avoid client-device clock as authoritative period source.
Period filters include inclusive start and exclusive end semantics internally to avoid boundary duplication.

72. Final Reporting Architecture Summary
The reporting system is a secure read layer over authoritative business data: fast dashboards serve daily decisions; detailed reports provide traceability; reconciliation reports prove consistency between invoices, payments, inventory, Khata, accounting and compliance; asynchronous exports prevent heavy workloads from blocking POS transactions; RLS/RBAC protect tenant and role boundaries; and versioned definitions keep historical metrics interpretable.
73. Important Production Note
Final statutory report layouts, GST fields, provider reconciliation formats, retention periods and legal reporting obligations must be validated against current authoritative requirements. Report performance must be tested using realistic production-scale data, and any aggregate/materialized view must have an explicit freshness and reconciliation policy.
